Tessera

Privacy Policy

Last updated 9 November 2026

The short version. Tessera does not collect anything about you. There is no account, no sign-up and no server belonging to Tessera. Everything you write stays in an encrypted database on your own phone. The developer cannot read your entries and has no way to obtain them.

Tessera is a personal journalling app for Android, built and maintained by Tlotlo Masisi. This policy explains, plainly, what the app stores, what leaves your device, and what it never does.

What Tessera stores, and where

Everything you enter — your daily entries, moods, dreams, money notes, bucket list, special dates and settings — is written to a database on your own device. That database is encrypted with SQLCipher, and its key is held in the Android Keystore, which is hardware-backed on most devices.

None of it is transmitted to the developer or to any third party. There is no Tessera server to transmit it to.

What leaves your phone

Three things can send data off your device. All three are triggered by you, and all three are described here in full.

1. Speech to text

If you use the Speak button to dictate an entry, Tessera first tries to turn your speech into text entirely on the device, using Android's offline speech recognition. On phones that support it, the audio never leaves your device.

If your phone cannot do this offline, Tessera stops and asks you, once, whether Android may instead send the audio to Google's speech recognition service to be transcribed. If you say yes, the audio of what you dictate is sent to Google and handled under Google's privacy policy, not this one. Your answer is remembered, and the app shows a VIA GOOGLE marker whenever it is using that route, so it never happens silently.

If you say no, dictation is disabled on that phone and you will not be asked again. You can still type.

Only the words you speak while dictating are involved. Entries you have already written are never sent anywhere.

2. Backups you create

Tessera can export your diary to a single encrypted backup file. That file is encrypted with a passphrase you choose, using PBKDF2 key derivation and AES-256-GCM, before it leaves the app.

Where it then goes is entirely your choice — your own storage, your own cloud drive, your own email. Tessera does not upload backups anywhere, and the developer never receives a copy.

There is no recovery. The backup passphrase is never stored and never transmitted. If you forget it, that backup cannot be opened — not by the developer, not by anyone. This is deliberate, and it is the trade-off for a backup nobody else can read.

3. The nightly reminder

If you set a nightly reminder, Tessera hands a pre-filled alarm to your phone's own clock app. No data about you is included and nothing is transmitted.

What Tessera never does

  • No account, no sign-up, no login to any service.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting SDK or usage tracking.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No location collection.
  • No contacts, photos or call log access.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information, because none is collected.

Permissions, and why

  • Microphone — only while you are actively dictating an entry. Nothing is recorded in the background and no audio file is saved.
  • Biometrics — to unlock the app with your fingerprint or face. The check is performed by Android; Tessera never sees your biometric data.
  • Notifications, exact alarms, run at startup — for the nightly writing reminder and so it survives a restart.
  • Set alarm — to hand a pre-filled alarm to your clock app.

Testing builds

Tessera is currently distributed to a small number of testers through Google Play's internal testing track. Google provides the developer with aggregate technical information such as crash reports and anonymous stability statistics. These come from Google, contain no diary content, and are governed by Google's privacy policy.

Children

Tessera is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Tessera removes the encrypted database with it. Any backup file you exported yourself remains wherever you put it, and only you can delete that.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever starts doing something this page does not describe, this page is updated first and the date at the top changes with it.
